This descriptive result narrows interpretation of the wider design language:
the eight local sessions do not test the value of actually exploring many new
institutions or architectures. They exercise only a small part of the permitted
space. The data do not identify whether initial representation, capability
documentation, model capacity, decoding, search budget or objective support
caused this concentration. The new information-interface documentation is a
prospective usability change, not a demonstrated remedy or proof that an
undocumented effect was unavailable.

The current count objective is already saturated on the realized reference
decisions, while other actions could still alter future submissions and state.
That conditional observation does not prove global optimality or that every
selection-source change is behaviorally inert. Actual source/input/outcome
comparisons and the queued native timing controls remain necessary to understand
the available dynamic effects. No novelty requirement, forced rewiring or hidden
merit gate is added to optimization.
