# V13 reviewed-production efficiency: explicit illustrative loss

Frozen before v13 actual inference, 2026-11-02. This is a new objective, not an
amendment of historical utility results or a claim about good national policy.

Minimize **L = -Q + 0.5 R**. Q is the sum of original integer-parsed native
accepted-paper review means; R is the number of actual review records across
accepted and rejected submissions. The existing native measurement extractor,
not generated institution scores, supplies both. Unreviewed acceptances have
zero Q. Zero reviews and rejection/acceptance of every paper are legal.

The coefficient is an explicit engineering preference, chosen prospectively to
make actual review workload an actionable loss component. It is not estimated
from data, a welfare weight, a validated quality measure or an extra validity
threshold. Unlike the historical `3.25 A-Q+.05 R`, this loss has no per-acceptance
charge and gives review work a larger declared weight. V13 compares it with
the unchanged `-A` throughput loss. Historical utility may be cross-scored only
as report-only data; it does not select candidates in either v13 arm.

This proxy can reward fewer reviewers and exploit noisy/optimistic native
scores. A high Q is not evidence of true scientific discovery. Those are
limitations to expose, not unannounced reasons to veto a loss-optimal system.
Post-hoc evaluation, technical legality and any future deployment policy remain
separate. No claim that the chosen coefficient resolves science-of-science
debates is made.
