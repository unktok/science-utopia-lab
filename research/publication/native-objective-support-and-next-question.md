# What the current objectives can distinguish

The current T/E experiments optimize declared engineering preferences, not a
universal definition of good science. Two observations narrow what their
outcomes can tell us and help choose the next important research question.
Neither changes current losses, candidates or ongoing evaluation.

## Selection under fixed records

Fix a set of submission decisions, their review outcomes and actual review
workload R. Let z_i denote whether record i is accepted and q_i its original
mean review score, with q_i=0 for an unreviewed record. Then

`T(z) = -sum_i z_i` and `E(z) = -sum_i z_i q_i + 0.5 R`.

For nonnegative q_i, accepting every record minimizes both conditional losses.
Zero-score acceptances tie under E. Thus different T/E coefficients need not
produce different acceptance rules. A per-acceptance charge would change this
conditional threshold, but adding one would define a different prospective
loss, not correct the current experiment.

This is not a global optimum for the native dynamic world: institutions can
change actual reviews, future submissions, information and resource trajectories.
The statement does not fix those effects or prove that a more complex policy
cannot help. It explains why accept-all is an essential strong control, and why
an improvement over the initial threshold rule alone is a weak research result.
The original E also changes when review panels change; its Q remains an
endogenous opinion, not independently verified scientific quality.

The final-activity component alone cannot rank these observed completed worlds.
This does not establish that activity is flat in all possible institutions,

conditioning on completion is not a method-level success estimate.

## Implications for the next main study

Execution feedback addresses whether a proposed program runs. It does not by
itself create an important scientific distinction between institutions. The
seven-world code-tested development loop therefore has a finite feasibility
role, not an automatic claim to close publication milestone R2.

Before a larger main comparison, inspect actual per-actor resource and project
trajectories, not only final aggregates, and identify a genuinely consequential
information/action/timing opportunity. Candidate questions include how freely
authored resource-sharing and information systems respond differently to output
and activity objectives under explicitly varied initial endowments or horizons.
The original Simulation, literature and persistent agents must remain intact.
Those are environment conditions, not hidden fairness constraints or a numerical
replacement world. Resource conservation is an execution law, while any social
preference belongs in the declared loss.

Compare generated institutions against strong simple policies with the same
actual authority. Do not make graph changes mandatory, artificially remove a
capable single-program baseline, or choose conditions merely to force different
winners. Additional losses or observer instruments require prospective meanings,
budgets and evaluation splits. None is frozen by this memo. The current620xx
results remain outside the code-tested designer inputs. The main-study choice
must integrate the closed actual comparisons rather than repeat an unchanged
weak question at larger scale.
