## Consequences for the first paper

- The method diagram must identify **what changes** (parameters, algorithms,
  mechanisms, wiring/process) separately from **how it is searched**. A generic
  generation/evaluation loop alone is not the contribution.
- The strongest available first-paper route is an executable design benchmark
  plus findings about how objectives and design freedom alter institutional
  structures and outcomes. This remains a hypothesis about contribution, not
  established significance or a guarantee of acceptance.
- The feedback pilot tests one part of that route. It does not replace the
  parameter-only/free-code/free-graph comparison or independent selected-system
  evaluation. Two generations are feasibility, not a sample-efficiency claim.
- Explain proxy exploitation as an observed mechanism, with outside evaluation
  kept separate from the optimization loss. Do not hide a candidate because it
  is normatively unattractive or silently replace the requested objective.
- Code readability is not automatically mechanistic interpretability. Trace
  which consumers executed changed rules and which scientist inputs/actions
  changed, using raw receipts and checkpoints.

The first three method patterns above are prior work we build on. Avoid claiming
“first automatic mechanism invention”, “first LLM+gradient optimization”, or
“optimized real science” on the basis of our implementation or this small pilot.

## Additional near-neighbor leads (abstract scope only)

The abstracts of [When Agents Evolve, Institutions Follow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27691)
and [PolicySim](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19649) were checked on2026-11-07.
The former studies executable multi-agent institution architectures; the latter
studies feedback-driven intervention search with adapted user simulators.
Full methods have not yet been compared here. Do not infer automatic institution
invention from the former's title, or claim priority over either from this note.
