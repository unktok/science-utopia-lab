# Adaptive diagnostic stop after the first closed numeric objective arm

This resource decision is made while V23 a1 is live, after its first several
planner/read-only responses and before its first closed two-generation arm.
The corrected first plan uses the proper loss sign, but steps0–4 again alternate
plans and unchanged parameter reads with no literal implementation edit. Do not
infer the remaining outcome or present this decision as preregistration.

Let the throughput arm close both generations and original-world measurements,
including its own-result feedback. At a verified between-arm boundary, stop
only the known owned writer before any second-arm author generation. If that
boundary has already been crossed, do not cancel in-flight inference or erase
an unfinished journal merely to capture it. Preserve all actual results/costs,
including any genuine different institution or worse outcome that appears.

Capture the boundary with a temporary owned-process pause, recheck that the
second arm is still queued and has no authoring directory, then immediately
deliver SIGINT/CONT to close the writer. Do not leave it paused while unrelated
work proceeds. If the check fails, immediately CONT and leave the pilot running.
Record authoritative writer termination separately from the original queue's
incomplete-budget state; do not rewrite queue statuses or mark unexecuted slots
as measured failures. Obtain two matching frozen-source/raw audits with explicit
incomplete-budget scope before further designer inference.

This is a debugging/resource-allocation decision, not a candidate gate or
objective change. Four original slots remain the declared maximum, but only
actual attempts are charged; unused slots are unexecuted, not refunded trials
or replacement outcomes. The full thread goal is unchanged. Inspect the actual
closed loop against it. If unmet, continue with a separately frozen/prospective
engineering revision and its own fresh actual readiness; if met, do not launch
more debugging trials merely because they were prepared.
