## Working paper reframed around objective-conditioned search

The paper was audited against the original Suto paper to ensure that the Lab
does not merely repeat fixed intervention comparisons. The durable distinction
is now recorded in Decision 0020: the optimization objective is the
experimental intervention, while the search path and resulting institution are
the primary outcomes.

The manuscript was reorganized accordingly. Its title and abstract now center
objective-conditioned black-box optimization; the introduction contrasts
matched counterfactual intervention studies with sequential institutional
search; the main study defines the three LossSpecs and asks path, design, and
cross-objective ranking questions. The first engineering campaign and the game
observatory were moved to appendices so they support rather than dominate the
research claim.

The revision also records negative evidence in the main interpretation. Three
objective labels yielded two distinct designs, the topic and burden arms had
only two valid candidate systems, and no equal-budget optimizer baseline or
optimizer-seed replication has yet been run. The v1.1 result is therefore an
existence proof for objective-conditioned institutional search, not evidence of
optimizer efficiency, repeatable path divergence, structural optimization, or
real-world policy quality.

The revised LaTeX built successfully into an 11-page PDF. All pages were
rendered to PNG and inspected for clipping, overlap, figure legibility, and

## Two-axis optimization continuum implemented

The apparent sequence from parameter optimization to structural optimization
to differentiable optimization was clarified into two axes. Parameters,
mechanism implementations, and node/edge topology are nested levels of design
freedom; black-box, differentiable-surrogate, and hybrid optimization are
proposal methods. Structural and differentiable therefore are not mutually
exclusive categories.

The first common implementation uses one six-family relaxed system template.
At the three executable freedom levels it exposes 6, 16, and 26 raw variables,
respectively. Gradient-free black-box proposals and freedom-masked gradient
proposals now cross the same deterministic projection, valid `SystemSpec`
fingerprint, declared loss, scenarios, and opaque outer evaluator. A hybrid
path uses a gradient result as the center of a result-dependent black-box
episode without treating surrogate score as simulator evidence.

The deterministic regression probe used eight gradient steps and four
black-box evaluations. At parameter, mechanism-configuration, and graph
freedom, black-box best outer losses were 1.133806, 1.133806, and 0.111956;
gradient projected outer losses were 1.133800, 1.133800, and 0.000056. A
three-evaluation structural hybrid retained 0.000056. These values are only
properties of the analytic synthetic fixture and do not rank real optimizers.

Thirty focused tests passed, including the existing differentiable and
black-box suites. The remaining integration gaps are schedule/latency in the
common relaxation and replacement of the synthetic evaluator with the compact
model-backed simulator. Decision 0022 records the architecture and claim
boundary.

## Generated institution programs and direct world gradients

The user requested concrete generation of new mechanisms and processes rather
than another catalogue of fixed implementations. The implementation now adds
a typed numeric AST with declared inputs/parameters/state/update rules and
one generic interpreter. Recursive synthesis, subtree mutation, custom-node
addition/removal, edge changes, delay and schedule mutation operate on actual
executable SystemSpecs. An empty institutional graph is valid, and generation
can introduce its first mechanism. The existing generic graph runner executes
these programs and reproduces checkpoint/resume exactly.

A new small recurrent numeric backend makes mechanism invocations, memory,
messages, resource allocation, production, learning and the selected LossSpec
one tensor computation. It relaxes program choice, node/edge presence, delay
and schedule, and projects the result to a discrete executable system. This
is direct differentiation of a miniature rollout, separate from the earlier
analytic synthetic surrogate and from the full original Qwen-agent simulator.

Implementation/protocol commit `c035d1da793dff2bb92383b2f7045ef78eb728f2` was
executed in an isolated clean detached experiment worktree. A numeric-only
run took 2.625 seconds. Qwen3-8B then generated SignalBoost and initial
attributes on its first validated response; the Qwen+numeric run took 16.458
seconds, including 12.995 seconds for the model request. Six researchers,
three periods, two development worlds and production/burden losses were used,
with five black-box proposals, 24 gradient updates and five hybrid proposals.

The resulting systems differ: production-gradient retains five nodes, the
production hybrid adds a sixth newly synthesized commons node, and
burden-gradient removes funding and changes execution order. Production versus
burden gradient systems yield mean production 0.185334 versus 0.090712 and
activity burden 3.824426 versus 0.166948. These are numeric-world proxies.
Burden's projection gap is 0.163754, so the relaxed score is not substituted
for the discrete observed loss. No unrequested policy floor was introduced.

All six gradient groups passed representative whole-rollout finite
