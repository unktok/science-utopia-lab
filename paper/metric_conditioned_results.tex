\section{Objective-Conditioned Institutional Search}
\label{sec:metric-conditioned-results}

The scientific intervention in this study is the optimization objective. The
institution is the dependent design object produced by search. The
preregistered study holds the simulator, eight-parameter design space,
\code{AdaptiveRandomSearch}, five-candidate budget, and seeds fixed while
changing only the \code{LossSpec}. It uses four-year longitudinal Qwen3-8B
worlds and three common development seeds per candidate. Each arm freezes its
winner before a common held-out suite evaluates all objective-conditioned
systems.

We ask three questions at the scale supported by this pilot:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
    \item Does changing the objective alter the observed optimization path?
    \item Does it alter the frozen institutional design?
    \item Do the frozen designs exchange rank when evaluated under other objectives?
\end{enumerate}
Whether adaptive search outperforms random, quasi-random, Bayesian, or
evolutionary search is a separate question that requires equal-budget optimizer
replication and is not answered here.

For each valid run $r$, the three minimized per-run losses are
\begin{align}
\ell^{\mathrm{part}}_r &= 1-a_r+0.05b_r, &
\ell^{\mathrm{topic}}_r &= -h_r, &
\ell^{\mathrm{burden}}_r &= b_r,
\end{align}
where $a_r$ is the active-researcher fraction, $b_r$ is review burden per active
researcher, and $h_r$ is topic entropy. Every arm aggregates its per-run loss
as the mean plus $0.25$ population standard deviations over the three
development seeds. The topic and burden objectives are deliberately narrow
stress tests: entropy does not measure truth or usefulness, and review burden
can be reduced by suppressing evaluation itself.

\subsection{Optimization-stage results}

The optimization phase is complete. All 45 logical design--seed rows reached a
terminal state: 30 were technically valid and 15 failed at the preregistered
reviewer-capacity boundary. Failed rows retained \code{loss: null}; no numeric
penalty, protected-slice veto, or undeclared promotion constraint entered the
optimizer. Twenty-nine rows were physically executed and 16 complete rows were
adopted from byte-identical initial trajectories under a hash-verified
provenance contract. The 45 physical process attempts consumed 29.99 serial
hours.

\subsection{What the search paths show}

The paths are informative but sparse. In the participation--review arm, C2
improved on C1 and became the incumbent; after C3 failed technical validity,
two valid adaptive proposals C4 and C5 were evaluated but did not improve it.
In the topic arm, C1 and C2 tied exactly and C3--C5 were invalid. In the burden
arm, C1 beat C2 and C3--C5 were invalid. Thus only one arm produced a multi-step
valid adaptive path. The experiment establishes that the loop can condition
subsequent proposals on arm-specific observations, but it does not yet show
rich or repeatable path divergence.

The participation--review objective selected a different system from the other
two objectives. Topic diversity and review burden selected the same system on
the development split, while the topic objective could not distinguish the two
valid initial systems. Accordingly, three objective labels yielded only two
distinct designs. This is evidence that changing the optimized objective can
alter the selected institution in this simulated setting; it is not evidence
that every objective induces a distinct institution or that adaptive search
beats non-adaptive candidate generation. The simultaneous winner seal was
written before any held-out registry existed.

\subsection{Interpretation: optimization, not another intervention sweep}

The substantive result is not that a particular acceptance rate, review
policy, or funding fraction causes a particular outcome. Those parameter
differences are properties of systems returned by search. The result is the
conditional map from declared objective to search history, frozen design, and
held-out profile. This is the dimension absent from a matched comparison of
hand-selected institutional interventions.

The current evidence supports only the first existence claim for that map. A
stronger optimization claim requires multiple optimizer seeds, a larger valid
budget, design-collapsed analysis when objectives choose the same system, and
equal-budget baselines such as random or Sobol sampling. This sealed study
changes eight parameters inside one fixed topology. The subsequent native
source/protocol/wiring experiments in Section~\ref{sec:native-design} broaden
the executable design object, but do not retroactively make this study a
structural-search experiment.
