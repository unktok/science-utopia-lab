\subsection{Expected-loss gradients without a differentiable native world}
\label{sec:native-estimators-v11}

The research objective is open institutional optimization, not making every
native operation differentiable. Let $X$ denote a categorical code/wiring
assignment and continuous latent institutional coordinates drawn from a
declared distribution $q_\theta$ within one externally generated vocabulary.
The original world produces native loss $L(X,\xi)$, with researcher-model
weights fixed but responses dependent on the installed institution. Under the
usual support, integrability and differentiation conditions, and no additional
direct $\theta$-dependence in the conditional environment,
\begin{equation}
 \nabla_\theta J
 = \mathbb E\!\left[L(X,\xi)\nabla_\theta\log q_\theta(X)\right],
 \qquad J=\mathbb E[L(X,\xi)].
 \label{eq:native-v11-score}
\end{equation}
This estimates a design-distribution gradient without differentiating native
execution. Its likelihood covers only explicit current-vocabulary choices and
Gaussian latents, not the unknown probability of arbitrary source invention
or opaque researcher text. Existing conditional score paths implement this
distinction; they are not whole-world pathwise derivatives.
Here $J$ is conditional on the supplied vocabulary and frozen source, model,
corpus, horizon and development-seed binding. Its expectation is over declared
institutional draws and any remaining conditional model randomness, not a
population-of-seeds average or the external author's generation distribution.

\paragraph{Optional representations and corrected control variates.}
LR and reparameterization estimators are related approaches to gradients of
expectations~\citep{parmas2021unified}. Composite estimators such as TPX and
composable propagation frameworks such as Proppo motivate exposing estimation
as a separate interface~\citep{parmas2023composite,parmas2022proppo}.
Nonsmooth-dynamics studies also distinguish differentiability from practical
estimator behavior~\citep{onoda2026differentiable}. These are methodological
references, not claims that their algorithms have been reproduced here.

For a surrogate $c(X)$ determined solely by sealed prior history, fixed for the
current scored batch and with tractable expectation, the corrected identity is
\begin{equation}
 \nabla_\theta J
 =\mathbb E\!\left[(L-c(X))\nabla_\theta\log q_\theta(X)\right]
   +\nabla_\theta\mathbb E_{q_\theta}[c(X)].
 \label{eq:native-v11-control-variate}
\end{equation}
An inaccurate $c$ need not bias this identity, but variance reduction is not
guaranteed. Fitting $c$ on the same scored batch, dropping the correction or
silently transporting it across incompatible code/coordinate semantics can
invalidate the intended estimator. Conversely, blending an approximate-world
derivative with a score estimate does not automatically eliminate its bias.

\paragraph{Shared open-design integration.}
V11 connects derivative-free feedback and prior-frozen corrected estimation
to the same free source/protocol/wiring boundary. Its protocol caps execution
at eight native attempts, two methods by two generations by two IID draws,
under the existing utility loss and unchanged five-author, two-year, seed-42
world. First-generation definitions are method-matched; later authoring and
fitting see only their own earlier outcomes. Existing differentiable
representations remain available, not prerequisites.

The optional, explicitly external-Codex-authored pilot example replaces the
previous feature/tensor/router chain with a Python review-evidence protocol
and a Python selection consumer connected to actual native publication. Its
categorical choice uses mean or minimum native reviewer scores; a Gaussian
latent maps to an evidence cutoff in $[2.75,4.25]$. Original review records,
not institution-authored final scores, determine $Q$ in the native loss.
The generic interface permits different code and graphs; this particular
pilot deliberately retains the two algorithms and wiring in generation~1
to keep the preceding-data control compatible. It does not demonstrate
autonomous source invention at every generation.

The score arm explicitly carries its learned logits and Gaussian mean into
the next version. The disclosed derivative-free author recenters on its own
best observed latent and increases that algorithm's logit by one relative to
the other, retaining the original distribution on an exact loss tie. This is
a simple loss-feedback heuristic, not Bayesian optimization. All candidates
and their original sampled probabilities are retained. Control coefficients
are a deterministic function of sealed earlier history and the fixed fitting
rule; later replay may recompute them, but no current-batch outcomes enter the
fit. These interfaces and their software tests do not establish variance
reduction, method superiority, autonomous invention or scientific value.

Generation~0 uses plain LR; its update defines the generation~1 distribution.
The prior-fitted control-variate update is computed after generation~1 and is
not followed by another native evaluation within this budget. Thus this pilot
tests that estimator on native observations, not the native performance of its
updated distribution. The final computed update and the last evaluated
distribution must remain distinct.
